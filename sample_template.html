What is being counted

Incidents (default)

BOCSAR's default unit is the criminal incident: activity involving the same offender(s) and victim(s), at one location, in one uninterrupted period, in one offence category. One police event can hold several incidents.

Victims

For murder and manslaughter BOCSAR counts victims, not incidents (marked with an asterisk in the source file). In this data: $m_n murder victims in $ty1. Victim counts exist only for crimes against the person.

Alleged offenders

Persons of interest proceeded against by police are a separate unit, held in a different BOCSAR dataset. Mixing offender counts with incident counts in one chart would mislead.

Source: BOCSAR, Using crime statistics (page last updated 5 Feb 2025; read $retrieved).

Counts versus population-adjusted rates

Why the denominator matters

NSW grew from $dn_a95 residents (June $fy0) to $dn_a25 (June $ty1), ABS ERP. For the single incident series $dn_offence, $dn_cnt incidents were recorded in $fy0: $dn_r95 per 100,000 with the $fy0 population, but only $dn_rfix if the single population printed in the BOCSAR file is used for every year. A fixed denominator understates this $fy0 rate by $under_s% and makes long-run declines look smaller than they are. This illustration keeps incident counts separate from victim-counted offences.

Vintages differ too

The BOCSAR file's "2025 population" column ($dn_b) is $gap_s lower than the ABS June $ty1 ERP ($dn_a25) used here. Rates are only comparable when every year uses the same population series, stated in the methods. Small areas and high-visitor areas (for example the Sydney LGA) need extra care because residents are not the population at risk.

Recording and definition breaks flagged

Automatically detected

    $flag_items

How the flags are used

  • NEW_SERIES: Coercive control ($cc_flag; $cc_n incidents in $ty1). A new offence has no meaningful long-run trend yet.
  • LEVEL_SHIFT: a year-on-year jump far outside the series' own variability (robust z of at least 3.5 and at least 50%). Each is checked against BOCSAR and ANZSOC notes before any trend is reported across it.
  • POLICING_SENSITIVE ($n_pol series): BOCSAR notes that drug offences, offensive behaviour and receiving stolen goods are mostly detected by police, so they track enforcement effort.
  • BOCSAR offence categories are built from NSW Police incident categories. National comparisons need ABS Recorded Crime - Victims, which codes offences to ANZSOC and counts victims (a person, premises, organisation or vehicle), so its figures are not interchangeable with BOCSAR incidents.

Reading the results

Safe to state

Direction and size of change in recorded rates, with confidence intervals, for a stated window and population series.

State with caveats

Offences affected by reporting willingness (sexual and domestic violence), policing activity (drugs, offensive conduct) or a flagged break.

Not supported

Claims about why a trend moved (a policy, an event) or about true offending levels. Trend analysis of recorded crime is descriptive, not causal.

SPSS equivalents (if you prefer SPSS output)

* Rate trend with population offset, over-dispersion via Pearson scale.
COMPUTE log_erp = LN(erp).
GENLIN count WITH year_c
  /MODEL year_c DISTRIBUTION=POISSON LINK=LOG OFFSET=log_erp
  /CRITERIA SCALE=PEARSON
  /PRINT FIT SOLUTION (EXPONENTIATED).
* 60-month monotonic trend test.
NONPAR CORR /VARIABLES=month_index count /PRINT=KENDALL TWOTAIL.

Caveats

  • Recorded crime counts what is reported to or detected by police and entered on COPS. Unreported crime is missing, and BOCSAR warns that for some offences recorded levels do not reflect actual crime.
  • Changes in willingness to report, policing priorities and recording practice can move the numbers without any change in offending.
  • Counting units differ (incidents, victims, alleged offenders); figures in different units are never added or compared directly.
  • Rates use June ERP for NSW residents; areas with large visitor populations or populations under 3,000 give unstable rates.
  • $ty1 is the last complete year; data run to $cov_last and the most recent months may be revised in later releases.
  • Trend estimates describe recorded data over the stated window. They do not show cause and effect.
